# Full-manuscript editorial report

Review date: 25 October 2026. Scope: all twelve Hinglish chapters. These are internal AI editorial reviews, not an independent human proofread or a guarantee of literary quality. Current source hashes are recorded in `REVIEW_RECORD.json`; changing a story file invalidates the builder's approval flag until review is renewed.

## Pass 1 — Developmental editing

Reviewed the full sequence against the story bible: Thursday 6 August begins Night One, Wednesday 12 August is Night Seven, and Thursday 13 August is the move. Chapters 11 and 12 advance to February and March 2027. The first two chapters occupy the first Thursday rather than consuming extra nights. Separation and the continuing legal process remain distinct; no home signature is said to create a divorce.

The full arc now includes a relapse after early warmth, a timed pause with an actual return, an apology that is heard without immediate forgiveness, a final-night discussion that does not cancel the move, and later lives that contain both increased competence and ordinary difficulty. The ending gives both an explicit choice to meet again without deciding to resume marriage. Their practical process is not silently cancelled by a tender coffee meeting.

Meera's publishing work stays consistent. Her rejected sample and negotiated small jobs show that asking clearly does not guarantee success. Aarav's training includes a public mistake, practical help and unfinished work rather than a sudden promotion. Family care remains mixed with expectations; neither parents nor friends explain the marriage for the couple.

Revisions: removed narrator comments that identified an incident as symbolic or not symbolic, announced a growth milestone, or explained how ordinary an action should feel. Developed the work-negotiation and class-error scenes because the earlier transition from intention to changed behaviour was too compressed. Removed the implication that self-assertion guarantees a new contract: the test edit is rejected, and replacement work remains less stable than the lost retainer.

Continuity reviewed: rental rather than owned flat; no home loan; Meera's new rent; modest savings and cancellable income; Aarav retains bed, fridge and table; shelf screws travel with panels; ordinary lift covers replace service-lift availability; medicines bag reaches Meera with the driver; extension board addresses the distant plug; towel remains for later return; carpenter repair survives Aarav's November move; green curtains arrive and are later shortened. Photograph is scanned before Meera takes the original.

## Pass 2 — Dialogue editing

Reviewed conversations for abstract question-and-answer structure, perfect speeches, role symmetry and voice. Aarav often begins with logistics, dates or a practical offer. Meera uses precise corrections and sharper humour when uncomfortable. Both can sound unfair. Neither immediately supplies a matching confession every time the other admits harm.

Kept the dinner argument uncomfortable, including Meera's 'hotel' remark and Aarav's defensive list. Accountability remains specific: the false departure message, failure to call, delayed gift, using a deadline to avoid a hospital visit. Aarav is allowed to remain hurt after hearing Meera's admission. Their next chapter does not present this as forgotten.

Revision: made the final-night reply about Meera staying less aphoristic and more hesitant. Preserved interruption, ordinary ordering, phone calls, household activity and incomplete answers. The last meeting includes a forgotten document and a brief defensive reaction; it is not proof that both have become ideal partners.

Remaining judgement call: a few direct emotional statements are intentionally retained at the final-night and final-meeting peaks. They follow concrete histories rather than opening a chapter as a lesson. An external reader may still prefer further trimming.

## Pass 3 — Hinglish copyediting

Reviewed Roman-script usage, pronouns, speaker paragraphs, grammatical agreement, dialogue tags and code-switching. Corrected 'guest room ki door' to natural darwaaza phrasing, 'coffee khilaayi' to 'coffee pilaayi', an awkward 'Neither' construction, and 'inside shelves' to 'andar ki shelves'. Conversational English work terms remain where natural. The first meeting's aap changes to tum with intimacy; parents receive respectful address.

Checks found no Devanagari mixed into the Roman-script body, no story placeholders and no duplicated paragraphs longer than twenty words. Those checks do not substitute for language review. The story word count excludes headings, scene dividers, message markers and all supporting material.

Spelling is guided by `HINGLISH_STYLE_GUIDE.md`. Roman-script Hinglish has legitimate variants; the manuscript chooses a house style rather than claiming a universal spelling standard.

## Pass 4 — Reader-experience editing

Reviewed all chapter openings and endings, the sequence of emotional pressure, and whether later scenes merely restate earlier insights. Objects recur through use rather than concluding every scene as metaphors. The move remains messy and administrative. The six-month chapter gives space to both people and shows setbacks, remaining habits, outside relationships and limited contact.

Revisions: replaced the chapter 10 closing explanation about behaviour without an audience with wet sleeves and the kitchen tap. Removed the final meeting's general observation about judging a person from one phone gesture. Removed a sentence telling readers that the library memory was not being interpreted. The ending now stays inside the chosen action: two separate people attend a reading together after explicitly allowing further contact and declining to promise an outcome.
